# Week 1 Phrase Sheet — Foundations

Characters are shown for reference only (menus/signs will use them) — the
actual goal is pinyin + audio, not character reading. For every phrase
below, don't just read the pinyin: look it up in **Forvo** (search the
characters, or the pinyin without tone marks) or **HelloChinese/Du
Chinese** to hear a native speaker say it, then shadow it per
`course-plan.md`'s method (listen 3-5x → shadow → record yourself).

## Tones primer (do this before anything else)

Mandarin syllables carry a tone that changes meaning. Same syllable `ma`:

| Tone | Mark | Example | Meaning | Contour |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1st | ā | mā (妈) | mother | flat, high |
| 2nd | á | má (麻) | hemp | rising |
| 3rd | ǎ | mǎ (马) | horse | dips low, then rises |
| 4th | à | mà (骂) | scold | falls sharply |
| neutral | a | ma (吗) | question particle | short, toneless |

Use Yoyo Chinese's free tone videos on YouTube for this — watching a
native speaker's pitch contour helps more than any written explanation.
Don't try to memorize tone rules yet; just get your ear used to hearing
the difference.

## Day 1 — Greetings

| 早上好 | Zǎoshang hǎo | Good morning |
| 晚上好 | Wǎnshang hǎo | Good evening |
| 再见 | Zàijiàn | Goodbye |
| 谢谢 | Xièxiè | Thank you |

## Day 2 — Courtesy & basic responses

| Characters | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 请 | Qǐng | Please |
| 不好意思 | Bù hǎoyìsi | Excuse me / sorry (casual, most common) |
| 对不起 | Duìbuqǐ | Sorry (for an actual mistake, stronger) |
| 没关系 | Méi guānxi | It's okay / no problem (reply to sorry) |
| 是 | Shì | Yes / is |
| 不是 | Bú shì | No / is not |
| 对 | Duì | Right / correct |

Note: in casual tourist situations, 不好意思 (bù hǎoyìsi) does double duty
as both "excuse me" (getting attention) and "sorry" — it's the one to
default to.

## Day 3 — Numbers 0-10

| Characters | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 零 | líng | 0 |
| 一 | yī | 1 |
| 二 | èr | 2 |
| 三 | sān | 3 |
| 四 | sì | 4 |
| 五 | wǔ | 5 |
| 六 | liù | 6 |
| 七 | qī | 7 |
| 八 | bā | 8 |
| 九 | jiǔ | 9 |
| 十 | shí | 10 |

Note: when counting *things* (two of something), 二 (èr) often becomes 两
(liǎng) — e.g. 两个 (liǎng ge) = "two [of something]". You'll hit this in
Week 2 when ordering food.

## Day 4 — Numbers 11-100 (the pattern, not new words)

Mandarin numbers are fully regular past 10 — no new vocabulary, just
combination:

| Characters | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 十一 | shíyī | 11 (10+1) |
| 十二 | shí'èr | 12 |
| 二十 | èrshí | 20 (2×10) |
| 二十一 | èrshíyī | 21 |
| 三十 | sānshí | 30 |
| 五十 | wǔshí | 50 |
| 九十九 | jiǔshíjiǔ | 99 |
| 一百 | yībǎi | 100 |

Pattern: `[tens digit] + 十 + [ones digit]`. Drill this by having a
native-audio source (ChinesePod has a numbers lesson) read random numbers
and saying them back before you see the answer.

## Day 5 — "I don't understand" / clarification

These are the single most useful phrases for the whole trip — you'll use
them constantly in the first week there.

| Characters | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 我听不懂 | Wǒ tīng bu dǒng | I don't understand (what you said) |
| 请再说一次 | Qǐng zài shuō yī cì | Please say it again |
| 请说慢一点 | Qǐng shuō màn yīdiǎn | Please speak a bit slower |
| 你会说英语吗? | Nǐ huì shuō Yīngyǔ ma? | Do you speak English? |

## Day 6 — Asking price / wanting things

| Characters | Pinyin | English |
|---|---|---|
| 多少钱? | Duōshao qián? | How much money? (how much does it cost) |
| 这个多少钱? | Zhège duōshao qián? | How much is this? |
| 这个 | Zhège | This (one) |
| 那个 | Nàge | That (one) |
| 我要... | Wǒ yào... | I want... / I'd like... |
| 我不要 | Wǒ bù yào | I don't want (it) |
| ...块 | ...kuài | ...yuan (colloquial, like "bucks") — used when stating a price |

## Day 7 — Review & integration

Recycle everything by shadowing this mini dialogue end to end, then
record yourself doing both parts:

> A: 你好! (Nǐ hǎo!) — Hello!
> B: 你好! (Nǐ hǎo!) — Hello!
> A: 这个多少钱? (Zhège duōshao qián?) — How much is this?
> B: 二十块。(Èrshí kuài.) — 20 yuan.
> A: 谢谢!再见! (Xièxiè! Zàijiàn!) — Thanks! Bye!

If you can do this from memory, fluently, at natural speed — without
reading — Week 1 is done. That's the bar; move to Week 2 (food) regardless
of whether every single phrase above feels perfect, since Week 2-4 will
keep recycling greetings/numbers/survival phrases anyway.
