# 4-Week Trip-Prep Course — Mandarin for Kunming & Lijiang

Goal: speak and understand enough Mandarin to handle greetings, food,
shopping, transport, and basic problems as a tourist. Not a general
Mandarin course — every phrase chosen earns its place by being useful on
this specific trip. No reading/writing characters; pinyin only, audio-first.

## Method (every day, ~20-30 min)

1. **Listen first.** Play the day's native-audio phrases 3-5 times before
   trying to say anything. Don't read pinyin while listening the first
   couple of passes — train the ear, not the eye.
2. **Shadow.** Play a line, pause, repeat it out loud immediately,
   matching rhythm and tone as closely as you can. Native audio only —
   TTS doesn't have real tone contours to imitate.
3. **Record yourself** on your phone saying the day's key phrases, and
   compare against the native clip. This is the single highest-value step
   for speaking — most learners skip it.
4. **Recycle yesterday.** Before new material, re-shadow 3-4 phrases from
   the previous day so nothing gets learned once and dropped.
5. **Live practice 2-3x/week** (see Week 4) — low-stakes conversation is
   what actually builds speaking confidence; passive listening alone won't.

## Resources (real native-speaker audio — not TTS)

- **Pimsleur Mandarin Level 1** — audio-only course, native speakers, built
  around shadowing. Best single resource for this plan's method; available
  via subscription or many public libraries (Libby/Hoopla).
- **Yoyo Chinese** — free YouTube series on pronunciation/tones, plus paid
  topic videos (native speaker, on-screen pinyin). Good for Week 1 tones.
- **ChinesePod** — graded dialogues by native speakers, searchable by topic
  ("ordering food," "shopping," "taxi") — good for Weeks 2-4.
- **HelloChinese / Du Chinese apps** — short native-audio recordings per
  phrase, good for quick drilling between sessions.
- **Forvo.com** — crowd-sourced native pronunciations of individual
  words/phrases; use to check any single word the apps above don't cover.
- **iTalki / Preply** — book 2-3 short lessons with a Mandarin tutor in
  Week 4 and roleplay the actual situations (ordering, bargaining, taxi).
  This is the speaking-practice piece nothing passive replaces.

## Week 1 — Foundations: tones, numbers, greetings, survival phrases

These are used every single day of the trip, so they come first and get
recycled through every later week.

- Pronunciation: the 4 tones + neutral tone; pinyin basics (Yoyo Chinese
  tone series)
- Numbers 0-100 (needed for prices, phone numbers, bargaining, ordering
  quantities)
- Greetings & courtesy: hello, thank you, you're welcome, please, sorry,
  goodbye, excuse me
- Survival phrases: "I don't understand," "please say it again," "please
  speak slowly," "do you speak English?," "how much is this?," "yes/no,"
  "I want... / I don't want..."

## Week 2 — Food: restaurants and street food

- Restaurant basics: table for N, menu please, I'd like..., what do you
  recommend, is this spicy, not spicy / less spicy, no cilantro/peanuts
  (if relevant), check please, this is delicious
- Street food basics: how much, one of these, can I get it to go, is it
  spicy, too hot (temperature vs. spice disambiguation)
- Numbers recap in context: ordering quantities, reading prices

## Week 3 — Shopping & money

- Asking price, "too expensive," "can you lower the price" (bargaining is
  normal in markets, less so in fixed-price shops — know the difference)
- Sizes, colors, "do you have this in...," "can I try it on"
- Payment: cash vs. mobile pay phrases (WeChat Pay / Alipay are near-
  universal; know how to ask "do you accept [cash/mobile pay]")
- Numbers recap: larger amounts, haggling exchanges

## Week 4 — Logistics, travel, and integration

- Directions: where is..., how do I get to..., is it far, turn
  left/right, straight ahead
- Transport: taxi phrases, "take me to...," bus/metro basics, "please
  stop here"
- Hotel: check-in phrases, "is breakfast included," "what time is
  checkout," wifi password
- Help & emergencies: "I'm lost," "I need a doctor/pharmacy," "please call
  for help," "where is the bathroom"
- **Integration**: mock full conversations chaining several days' phrases
  (e.g., taxi → hotel check-in → asking for restaurant recommendation →
  ordering). Book 2-3 live tutor sessions this week and run these as
  roleplay — this is the proof that passive listening turned into actual
  speaking ability.

## Notes

- Yunnan (Kunming, Lijiang) has local languages/dialects (e.g. Naxi in
  Lijiang), but Mandarin is universally understood for tourism — no need
  to prep anything local-language-specific.
- Keep a phrase list on your phone as backup, but the point of this plan
  is to not need to read it — listening comprehension and spoken recall
  under light pressure.
